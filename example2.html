RULE 01: DEFINITION
02/10
CORE PRINCIPLE

Suspense is Not Danger

THE PSYCHOLOGY

Danger is when someone points a gun. Suspense is the anticipation.

If an attacker attacks on page one, viewers feel shock, not suspense. Suspense requires a gap in knowledge: a character needs clarity, but cannot verify reality.

RULE 02: RESTRICTION
03/10
COMPLICATION

Trap the Character with Reality

GROUNDED LIMITATION

Do not force supernatural coincidences or cartoonish bad luck.

Introduce a routine flaw everyone experiences. A dead battery, a broken latch, or bad reception. Relatable limits make audiences imagine themselves in the exact position.

RULE 03: PACING
04/10
URGENCY TRIGGER

Start the Unforgiving Clock

RESOURCE DRAIN

Tension peaks when safety drains away in plain sight.

The audience must see the finish line closing. Give them one vital asset with a visible countdown. Every passing second of screen time must cost the character something irreversible.

RULE 04: INFORMATION
05/10
THE PIVOT

Cut the Lifeline at the Target

KILL CERTAINTY

Amateur writers resolve the tension too quickly.

If the character reads the license plate number, the mystery evaporates. Cut their connection two seconds before they memorize the crucial detail. Turn safe tools into dead bricks.

RULE 05: AMBIGUITY
06/10
THE DILEMMA

Turn Relief into Suspicion

CONTRADICTING SIGNALS

The solution arrives, but without proof of safety.

When an answer presents itself, make it look partially right and potentially catastrophic. The human mind naturally panics when forced to choose between isolation and unknown risk.

RULE 06: VISUAL BEATS
07/10
SUBTLE DETAILS

Use Mechanical Micro-Actions

BEHAVIOR OVER WORDS

Stop using internal dialogue to explain how someone feels.

Show physical hesitations: a hand hovering over a cold door handle, brake lights reflecting in puddles, a tinted driver window rolling down halfway. Let physical actions deliver the pressure.

RULE 07: CHOICE
08/10
THE CLIMAX POINT

Force an Unforgiving Choice

NO EASY OUT

Suspense breaks if the protagonist can simply step away.

Option A carries extreme danger. Option B guarantees immediate exposure. If walking away is safe, the scene has zero stakes. Force an immediate decision where both outcomes carry risk.

THE ENGINE

The Everyday Suspense Loop

THREE STEPS

Keep this exact formula handy whenever drafting a thriller beat.

1. Establish a simple routine need.
2. Strip away the character's verification tool.
3. Deliver an ambiguous answer they are forced to trust.
